Dear Vireo Data Hiring Team,

Vireo is making a multi-tenant analytical store behave predictably for tenants whose workloads have nothing in common with each other, the same problem I have spent the last four years solving at Tessera Labs' scale. I would like to bring that experience to bear at yours.

At Tessera Labs I am the technical lead for the storage platform across three sub-teams. I designed the sharding strategy for our timeseries write path, which now sustains 1.4M QPS at p99 38 ms over a 19 PB dataset. I also own the published service contracts for the fourteen components other teams build against. The part of that work I would bring to Vireo is not the number; it is the habit of writing the trade-off down. We chose CRDT metadata sync over Raft because losing a quorum cost our operators four seconds of unavailability every time, and accepting eventual convergence was cheaper than paying that on call.

Before Tessera I led the cold-start program for Helix Cloud's isolate runtime and took boot time from 380 ms to 18 ms at p95 across the fleet. I then rewrote the regional autoscaler to lift a per-region ceiling from 8K to 110K concurrent functions. Both projects required me to own the runtime and the control plane that schedules it at once, because the boot path could not be tuned without changing how the scheduler placed work. I also wrote the error-budget policy and ran the review that kept those latency numbers from regressing after the project closed.

Vireo's storage engine is already proven, and the multi-tenancy work around it is early enough that one engineer can still own consistency, isolation, and cost per tenant as a single problem rather than three separate ones. That is the work I want to do next. I would welcome the chance to hear where it stands today.
